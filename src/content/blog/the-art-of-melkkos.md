Growing up, ‘melkkos’ was this mysterious, off-limits staple in Afrikaans homes that I was forbidden from experiencing. Every time I begged my mother to make it, she simply responded with those famous words: “You won’t like it.” I dropped it, arguing with her was a losing battle. 

Looking back, she lied. Pure, calculated deception. She knew that if I tasted it, I would demand it weekly - so instead, she made me the ‘sago’ version, because that’s better!

So what is melkkos, and why I am so dedicated to it that I am dedicating my first ‘recipe’ blog article to it. Well it is essentially a runny milk tart with deliberate lumps, served hot with cinnamon sugar. Yip, that’s it - nothing fancy, but something that stole my heart.

The true history of melkkos is rather vague, some would argue that it evolved from European dairy traditions like Dutch melkspijs, German Milchsuppe, or even Nordic milk soups. Regardless of its origins, broken down on its core - melkkos is milk infused with spices like cinnamon, star-anise, cloves, etc. and a thickening starch. 

## Pick your starch-base

If you leave through old, grease-stained copies of the Kook & Geniet and other similar recipe books - you will notice everyone used terms like melkkos, melksnysels, and frummels rather loosely. So your first job is to determine, which starch-base floats your boat:

- **Frummel (The Crumb Method):** You rub cold butter into flour until it forms rough, pea-sized crumbs, which is dropped into the infused milk. This is my favorite base.
- **Melksnysels (The Noodle Method):** A stiff egg-and-flour dough rolled paper-thin, sliced into ribbons, and boiled in the infused milk.
- **Kluitjies (Drop Dumplings):** A loose batter dropped into the infused milk pot, using a spoon.
- **Sago (Tapioca):** Jelly-like, translucent pearls… If you want to find out more Google it!

It took me way too long to realize the magic isn’t just the starch - it’s all in the liquid base. Once you start infusing whole spices directly into the milk before adding the starch, melkkos stops being struggle-food and turns into an actual delicacy.

There was just one problem: my husband hates cinnamon. So to keep the peace in the house, I pivoted to Cape Malay aromatics: star anise, cloves, cardamom, and a little dark cacao powder. 

## THE FORMULA (DON'T PANIC, IT'S BASIC MATH)

> For the chocolate crumbs, just remember the 2x rule: Double the butter, flour, and cocoa relative to the milk unit.

> 1 Cup Milk  --->  2 Tbsp Butter + 2 Tbsp Cacao Powder + 2 Cups Flour

Crucial rule: Kill the stove heat before dumping in your dry mix. Boiling milk holds more than enough residual heat to cook the krummels. Stir slowly. Maintain the integrity of the lump.
